## Plain-language note on "background-referenced" and "as provided"

A phase map stores, for every pixel, how much the light was delayed. The delay of empty background should be zero, but
the reconstruction leaves an arbitrary constant on top, and that constant differs from image to image and between the two
file groups. **As provided** means the numbers exactly as they are in the files, constant included. **Background-referenced**
means subtracting each image's own empty-background level so that background is 0 rad and a cell's integrated phase measures
only the cell. Contrast and gradient loss terms do not care about the constant. The integrated-phase term (IPP) and the
IPE metric, and of course dry mass, do care: a cell's integrated phase is `S = sum(phi)`, and a constant `c` adds `c x area`
to it. The original 8-bit pipeline used whatever grey levels the per-image stretch produced (so it was not background-referenced
for the loss or IPE; only the dry-mass step subtracted a background), which is why the manuscript says no background
correction is applied there.
