| ms ¶6 | text | Digital holographic microscopy enables label-free quantitative phase imaging of biological cells and provides phase-derived biophysical information, including cellular dry mass. However, conventional AI-based segmentation methods are typically optimized using geometric overlap losses that do not account for the quantitative phase information enclosed by predicted cell boundaries, potentially introducing errors in downstream biophysical measurements. In this study, we propose a physics-aware lightweight segmentation framework that treats the predicted cell boundary as a quantitative measurement domain rather than solely as a geometric contour. A Physics-Aware Phase Consistency Loss is introduced by combining phase-mask contrast, boundary-gradient alignment, and integrated-phase preservation constraints to explicitly preserve the quantitative phase integrated within the segmented cell region. The framework employs Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) to efficiently adapt lightweight segmentation architectures to red blood cell (RBC) quantitative phase images. Over three training seeds, EdgeSAM at LoRA rank 32 (the primary configuration) achieved a Dice of 0.738 ± 0.006, an AJI of 0.335 ± 0.020, and a boundary F1-score of 0.642 ± 0.005 and retained all four annotated classes in every seed, whereas MobileSAM at rank 8 (the sensitivity comparison) reached a Dice of 0.810 ± 0.006 with lower instance separation (AJI 0.262 ± 0.017); EdgeSAM at rank 8 lost one class in one of three seeds and MobileNet-UNet lost two classes in every seed.
